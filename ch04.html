3. Will they test calculus or linear algebra in 15 minutes?

Very unlikely. 15 minutes with two people is just enough for introductions, your research and a few questions. No source reports maths at a lab interview. NAIST tests maths formally on 10 March.

What is possible: one or two concept questions linked to your plan. These come from Dr. Ji's side (causality) and are already in your kit:

  • "What is your treatment, and what could confound it?" (question bank C24, D10)
  • "Why logistic regression? What are random effects?" (C22, D7, D8)
  • "Is 3 runs enough?" (C25)

If something like this comes, answer in plain words. If you don't know, say: "I have not studied that properly yet. My plan is to take a statistics course in my first semester."

4. Minute by minute (best guess)

Time What happens What you do
0:00 to 1:00 Hello, they may introduce themselves Smile, "Thank you for your time." Camera on, no notes in hand.
1:00 to 2:00 "Please introduce yourself" 1-minute self-intro (scripts file, section 2). Not the 3-minute one.
2:00 to 4:00 "Tell us about your research" 2-minute pitch (scripts file, section 4). If they say "briefly", use the 1-minute version under it. No slides unless they ask.
4:00 to 12:00 Their questions, probably 4 to 6 Short answer first, one example, stop. See section 5.
12:00 to 14:00 "Do you have any questions?" Ask one question (section 6). Two only if time allows.
14:00 to 15:00 Next steps "Thank you very much. I look forward to hearing from you."

The biggest risk in 15 minutes is talking too long. A 3-minute answer uses 20% of your interview. Time your intro and pitch with a stopwatch.

5. The questions most likely in 15 minutes

Ranked by how likely they are. Model answers are in 03_Research_Plan_Defense/05_question_bank_with_model_answers.md (IDs in brackets).

Fit and motivation (almost certain)

  1. Why this lab? Which of our papers did you read? (A5, A7) Name L1 Manifests and L2 Agent READMEs: Dr. Reid co-authored both.
  2. What exactly is new compared with our papers and Gloaguen et al.? (C39, C40, C48, C49) Never say "nobody has done this". Say what is different.

Dr. Reid's likely angle (context-file papers, mining GitHub, tools and user studies)

  1. How will you choose repositories and rules? How do you build a task? (C13, C14)
  2. Have you mined GitHub or used AIDev? (B19, D5)
  3. How will you evaluate your Year 2 tool? (C55)

Dr. Ji's likely angle (causality, LLM agents)

  1. What is causal in your design and what is only association? (C24) Key line: "The file version is the treatment. Every task runs with every version, so for RQ1 and RQ3 I compare like with like. Specificity and linter coverage differ between rules, so those parts are associations."
  2. Isn't RQ3 circular? Why compare file versions, not runs that complied? (C21, C23)
  3. What about interactions between rules? (C27) Mention that his CAM paper warns effects can be non-additive, and that one-at-a-time removal cannot see interactions.

About you (likely, quick)

  1. Your skills: languages, how you use coding agents (B17, B18). Never say agents write most of your code.
  2. April or October 2027? Your job? Funding? Japanese? (A10 to A13) Your answers are in section 7.

6. What to ask them (pick one)
