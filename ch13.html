Part 1: Before Monday (23 to 26 Oct)

Chapter 13 of 30

Day-of checklist: the lab pre-application interview

When and how long: Sunday, about 15 minutes: camera, mic, internet, backup.

What this file is: everything practical for the day of the online interview: tech setup, time zones, what to keep on screen, dress, the first 60 seconds, the closing, and the thank-you email.

The platform, length and exact format of this lab interview are UNKNOWN until the scheduling email arrives. Prepare for Zoom, Microsoft Teams, Google Meet and Webex. (NAIST's official math exam guideline video asks for "A device compatible with Webex" [V], but the lab may use something else.)

1. Time zone

Rules:

  • Always write both: "10:00 JST (6:30 IST)".
  • When you get a calendar invite, open it and check that your calendar shows it in IST at the right time.
  • Their "morning" is your early morning. Greet them by their time of day, or just say "Hello".
  • Japanese public holidays in this period (fixed-date national holidays, Culture Day and Labour Thanksgiving Day): 3 Nov 2026 (Tuesday) and 23 Nov 2026 (Monday). Do not offer slots on these days. Also avoid weekends.
  • Block 10:30 to 11:00 IST on Monday 26 Oct with Layerpath. Do not take the call at your work desk.

2. Tech setup

2.1 Two days before

  • Install the app they named (Zoom, Teams, Meet or Webex) on the laptop and on your phone. Update both to the latest version.
  • Sign in, and set your display name to "Sudhanshu Pandit". Your emails come from your brother's address, so make sure nothing on screen says "Rishabh".
  • macOS: give the app permission for Camera, Microphone and Screen Recording (System Settings, Privacy and Security). macOS asks you to restart the app after giving Screen Recording permission. Do it now, not during the call.
  • Do a test call (Zoom has a test meeting; for Teams or Meet, call a friend). Check camera, microphone, speaker and screen sharing.
  • Test sharing one window (the proposal PDF), not the whole screen.
  • Prepare the files you might share (section 4) and open them once to check they open fast.

2.2 Camera

  • Camera at eye level. Put the laptop on books or a box.
  • Frame: head and shoulders, a little space above your head.
  • Look at the camera when you speak, not at their faces on the screen. Put their video window near the top of the screen, just under the camera.
  • Background: a plain, tidy wall. No virtual background (it can glitch, and in the official exam they may ask you to show the room [V]).

2.3 Microphone and sound

  • Use wired earphones with a microphone, if you have them. Bluetooth can lag or run out of battery.
  • Test your voice level in the app's audio settings. Turn off any "noise cancellation" that cuts your words.
  • Phone on silent, and away from the laptop (it causes buzzing).

2.4 Lighting

  • Light in front of you: face a window or a lamp. Not behind you.
  • If the call is early morning IST (for example 6:30 IST), it may still be dark. Test the lighting at that exact time the day before.

2.5 Internet and power

  • Use the strongest connection: Ethernet if possible, or sit close to the router.
  • Ask family not to stream video or download big files during the call.
  • Backup internet: your phone hotspot, tested once with the laptop. Phone charged above 80%.
  • Backup device: the meeting app on your phone, signed in, with the meeting link ready.
  • Laptop plugged in and charged. If power cuts happen in your area, keep the router on an inverter or UPS if you have one. If you do not, the phone hotspot above is your backup.
  • Have a short email ready on your phone, to send if you drop out: > Subject: Reconnecting now (Sudhanshu Pandit, 2027M3055) > Dear Dr. Ji and Dr. Reid, I am sorry, my connection dropped. I am reconnecting now [or: I am joining from my phone now]. Sudhanshu

2.6 Quiet room

  • A room with a door you can close. Tell your family the exact time and length, and ask for no interruptions.
  • A small sign on the door: "Interview, please do not disturb, until 11:15 am".
  • Fans, air conditioner and TV off if they are noisy.
  • No other person in the room. (The official exam requires this [V]. Practise the same way.)

2.7 Notifications off

  • macOS Focus or Do Not Disturb on.
  • Quit Slack, WhatsApp, Mail, messaging apps, Claude and ChatGPT apps, and any work tools.
  • Pause MaxMi and any screen capture or recording tool you run. Their screen should not be captured by your own software.
  • Close all browser tabs except what you need.

4. What to keep on screen

The lab interview has no stated rule about materials [UNKNOWN]. Still, do not read from notes: they will see your eyes move. And practise without notes, because the official exam in March allows no materials in sight [V].

On the laptop (open but hidden behind the meeting window):

  • The meeting window, large, near the top of the screen under the camera.
  • Your proposal PDF (01_Your_Documents/Research_Proposal_NAIST_Sudhanshu_Pandit.pdf) at 100% zoom, Fig. 1 easy to find. Only share if asked or invited.
  • The 6 slides from 02_self_intro_and_research_pitch_scripts.md, ready in presentation mode, plus a PDF copy. Only share if invited.
  • Optional: your GitHub page (github.com/mafex11/MaxMi) in one tab, in case they ask to see code.

On the desk (off camera):

  • One index card, nothing more:
    • interviewers' names and titles: Dr. Zhenlan Ji and Dr. Brittany Reid, assistant professors;
    • key numbers: about 10 repos, about 30 rules, 5 versions, 2 agents, 3 runs, at most 1,800 runs, about 1,300 to 1,900 USD;
    • your 3 questions to ask (from 05_questions_to_ask_them.md);
    • your one-line answers: start date, job, funding, Japanese.
  • Paper and pen, to write down questions, names and papers they mention.
  • A glass of water.

Not on screen: the scripts, the question bank, any chat window, any AI tool.

If they ask you to share your screen:

  1. "Sure. I will share the proposal PDF." (or the slides)
  2. Share the window, not the whole screen.
  3. Make the text large. Ask: "Can you see it clearly?"
  4. Stop sharing when you finish, and say so.

5. Dress

  • A plain, collared shirt in a solid, light colour (light blue, white or grey). No logos or slogans.
  • Smart trousers too, in case you have to stand up.
  • No suit or tie needed. For the official exam NAIST says there is no need for formal clothes and you can wear something comfortable [V], though one applicant noticed the examiners wore suits [A]. A neat collared shirt is the safe middle.
  • Hair neat. Glasses clean, if you wear them.

6. The first 60 seconds (script)

When the call starts (about 10 seconds):

"Hello, good afternoon. Thank you very much for taking the time to meet me today. Can you hear me clearly?"

It is 14:00 in Japan for them, so say "good afternoon", even though it is morning for you.

When they introduce themselves (about 10 seconds):

"Thank you. It is very nice to meet you, Dr. Ji and Dr. Reid."

Write their names on paper as they say them. If Prof. Iida or Dr. Kashiwa join: "Thank you for joining, Professor Iida."

If something is wrong with sound or video:

"Sorry, I think my audio is not clear. Let me fix it." (Check the selected microphone. If it still fails within 30 seconds, rejoin.)

When they ask you to introduce yourself (about 60 seconds):

  • Use the 1 minute intro from 02_self_intro_and_research_pitch_scripts.md. Then stop and wait.

You have not corrected the CV in an email (you did not attach it to the scheduling reply), so add one short line after your intro:

"May I correct one thing on the CV I sent first? It said my Japanese is 'Limited Working'. That was a mistake. I am a beginner, below N5, and I am learning. I can send the corrected CV today."

Do not:

  • Start with a long speech before they lead.
  • Start in Japanese.
  • Apologise for your English. It is good.

7. Closing (script)

When they ask "Do you have any questions for us?"

  • Ask 2 or 3 questions from 05_questions_to_ask_them.md. Fewer if time is short. Always ask about next steps.

Your closing words (about 30 seconds):

"Thank you very much for your time and your questions today. I learned a lot, especially from your comment about [one specific thing they said]. I would really like to do this research in your laboratory. Could you tell me what the next step is, and whether I should prepare or send anything?"

After they answer:

"Thank you. I will [repeat the next step in your words]. Thank you again, and goodbye."

  • Smile and wait. Let them end the call. If everyone waits, say "Thank you" once more and leave.
  • Optional, only if it feels natural: "Arigatou gozaimashita."

Do not ask:

  • "Will I be accepted?" or "How did I do?"
  • About pay or money amounts, unless they raise funding first. (The IPGP scholarship question is fine: see 05_questions_to_ask_them.md.)

8. Right after the call

Within 30 minutes, write down:

  • Every question they asked, in their words as far as you remember.
  • What you answered, and what you would say better next time.
  • Every paper, tool or idea they mentioned. Read the papers within a week.
  • Any next step or deadline they gave, and who said it.

Why: two past applicants reported that questions from their lab meetings came back in the official exam [A].

9. Thank-you email: timing

  • Send it the same day, within a few hours of the call.
  • Best if it arrives during their working day. Before 18:00 JST (14:30 IST) is ideal [INF]. If the call ended late in their day, sending it the same evening IST is still fine.
  • Reply on the same email thread (Reply All), so the same people see it. Do not add new people.
  • Keep it short: about 100 words. Mention one specific thing from the conversation.
  • Template: 04_email_templates.md, email 2.
  • If you promised to send something (a corrected CV, a link, a document), attach it to this email.

10. If something goes wrong

Problem What to do
Your internet drops Switch to the phone hotspot and rejoin. If it takes more than 1 minute, send the "Reconnecting now" email from your phone.
The laptop freezes Join from your phone. Apologise once, briefly, and continue.
Power cut Laptop battery keeps you going. Switch to the phone hotspot if the router dies.
You cannot hear a question "Sorry, could you say that again, please?"
Someone walks into the room "Excuse me for one second." Close the door, come back, continue.
They are late Wait 10 minutes in the meeting. Then send a short, polite email: "I am in the meeting room and will wait. Please let me know if another time is better."
You are late (should not happen) Email immediately with a one-line apology and the time you can join.
