Chapter 29 of 30

Intel report: the SD lab pre-application interview, the official NAIST exam, and funding

When and how long: Look up only when needed: sources, official exam rules, funding.

Checked on 21 Oct 2026. Every claim has a source key (S = official or primary page, A = one applicant's personal account). The full URL list is at the end.

How to read the tags:

  • [V] Verified. I read it myself on an official NAIST page, the SD lab site, or a NAIST PDF. Exact quotes are in quotation marks.
  • [A] Anecdotal. One past applicant's blog. It may be out of date or true only for that lab or year.
  • [INF] My inference or arithmetic. Not stated by any source.
  • UNKNOWN. No public source found. I say so plainly.

0. The seven things that matter most

  1. Time-critical funding item. NAIST's own MEXT scholarship route for Information Science (IPGP) is open now: the application period is 14 Oct to 13 Nov 2026. You apply through your prospective supervisor, and they must write a Letter of Acceptance. It pays 144,000 yen a month (the guidelines add that the amount "is subject to change depending on the annual budget"), plus tuition and entrance fee exemption, for October 2027 entry only. There are 4 master's places for the whole division. [V] (S19) While your IPGP application is open, you must not apply for JASSO or other MEXT support (rule III). See section 5.3. Raise it with the lab early, ideally when you reply to the scheduling email, not only at the interview.
  2. Who you will meet. Prof. Iida wrote: "Our assistant professors will contact you soon to arrange pre-application interview." The lab's member page lists exactly two assistant professors: Brittany Reid and Zhenlan Ji. [V] (S12, local file 01_Your_Documents/Prof_Iida_reply_21Oct.txt). Kashiwa is the associate professor and may join. [INF]
  3. The lab interview content is UNKNOWN for this lab. No public account of an SD lab pre-application interview exists that I could find. Section 2 lists what is known from other NAIST labs and from NAIST rules.
  4. Math or coding at the lab interview: UNKNOWN. No source says the SD lab tests math or live coding before the exam. The lab says programming skill is "not mandatory before admission". [V] (S10) The official exam does test math orally. [V] (S1)
  5. What you say at the lab interview can come back in the official exam. Two applicants wrote that questions from their meetings with the lab professor were asked again in the real exam. [A] (A1, A2)
  6. The official exam is a 20 minute online interview in English, with oral math (one algebra, one analysis problem, about 12 minutes) and questions on your proposal. Interview plus essay is 90 of 200 points; math is 30. [V] (S1, S5, S7, S8)
  7. Funding questions get asked. In one English-language exam (3rd round, 2021 exam for 2022 entry) the examiners asked: "Who will provide you with financial support? Are you going to support yourself?" [A] (A6). Have a short honest answer ready (section 5.8).

  • Programming conversation does come up in official exam interviews [A]:

    • "コードは書けるか，またその言語は？" (can you write code, which language), "Javaはどれくらいできるのか" (how good is your Java), "オブジェクト指向言語とは，またそのメリットは？" (what is OOP, its merits). (A4)
    • "使う予定の解析モデルの仕組みを知っているか" (do you understand how the analysis model you plan to use works). (A3) For you, this means: be able to explain a mixed-effects logistic regression in plain words.

Prudent preparation [INF]: be ready to talk through code you wrote (Yuki first, then Magic Editor) and how you would script a compliance checker. Be ready for one or two light math or statistics questions, since the official exam has oral math and Ji works on causality.

2.6 Red flags to avoid

Each item says where it comes from.

  1. A vague "why this lab". If your idea could be done anywhere, expect "then you don't need our lab". [A] (A2) Tie your plan to their papers by name, and to Kashiwa's FOREST theme. [INF]
  2. Not reading what they point you to. If they mention a paper, read it before the exam. It may be asked again. [A] (A1, A2)
  3. Claims you cannot defend. NAIST's essay advice: 「面接時にはそれを自分の言葉ではっきりと説明できるように準備しておくこと」 (be ready to explain it clearly in your own words at the interview). [V] (S8). One applicant warns that people with well-written essays failed because they could not answer. [A] (A1)
  4. Inconsistent documents. The CV Prof. Iida has says "Japanese (Limited Working)" by mistake. The 2027 Guide warns that falsified or fabricated information can be treated as a "fraudulent act". [V] (S1). Correct the CV line yourself, early and briefly: "I am a beginner, below N5. My CV had an error, and here is the corrected version." [INF]
  5. Overclaiming novelty. Their own papers, Gloaguen, Guardrails and Harness-IF already cover parts of this. See .context/verified_dossier.md section (b). Say what is new in one sentence and admit the overlaps. [INF]
  6. Treating BrandMemory as evidence. It is one comparison on one demo. Say so before they do. [INF, from brief]
  7. Unclear commitment. Full-time job, unclear start date, unclear funding. Have one-line answers ready (section 5.8 and section 4). [INF]
  8. Not asking anything. The lab invites questions about current research and themes. [V] (S10)
  9. Not understanding the examiner and staying silent. One English-exam applicant could not catch a word and had to ask several times; he advises talking with the lab faculty in English beforehand to get used to their speech. [A] (A6) It is fine to say "Sorry, could you say that again?" [INF]

2.7 Good questions to ask them [INF]

  • Does the lab support an IPGP MEXT application this year? Would Prof. Iida write the Letter of Acceptance? (deadline 13 Nov 2026, see 5.3)
  • Would you advise April 2027 or October 2027 entry for me?
  • Which parts of my plan overlap with work the lab is doing now that is not public yet?
  • Is there budget for model API costs in agent experiments, or should the design use open-weight models?
  • Are there RA positions on the FOREST project for master's students? (S14 says the project includes "additional support for Research Assistants (RAs)")
  • Is an online internship before enrollment possible? (The lab says online internships are possible; S11)

3. The official entrance exam

3.1 Format: exact quotes [V]

From the 2027 Guide (S1, local copy 01_Your_Documents/NAIST_official/NAIST_2027_Masters_Admission_Guide.pdf, section 7):

  • "The examination will consist of a 20 minute interview in Japanese or English, depending on the language selected at the time of application registration."
  • "In principle, interviews are conducted remotely using an online conferencing system."
  • Information Science:
    • "(i) Oral examination on questions of mathematics (algebra, analysis, etc.)"
    • "(ii) Questions regarding the research proposal and areas of information science relevant to the examinee's area of specialization."
    • "Make sure that no materials are in sight during the interview."
  • Math scope:
    • "Algebra: From Chapter 1 to Chapter 7 of the following book. Gilbert Strang, Introduction to Linear Algebra, Fourth edition, Wellesley-Cambridge Press."
    • "Analysis: From Chapter 1 to Chapter 15 of the following book. Serge Lang, A First Course in Calculus, Fifth edition Springer."
  • Biological and Materials Science have a "5-minute oral presentation". Information Science does not list one. [V] (S1)
  • If applicants reach "twice the number of admission capacity", there may be a document-based "preliminary screening". [V] (S1)
  • Research proposal: "Write in English or Japanese. The language that you will be using throughout the examination." [V] (S1)

3.2 The math part [V]

  • "The examiners will show on-screen two math problems (one from Algebra and one from Analysis). They will ask you to solve and explain your solutions within 12 minutes." [V] (S7)
  • "you will have neither a 10-minute preview time (before the interview) nor multiple questions to choose from." [V] (S7)
  • "Please write the answer on a sketchbook in front of the camera and explain it." [V] (S5)
  • Level: 「理系の学部1・2年で習う数学です」 (math taught in the first two years of a science degree). [V] (S8)
  • How it is graded: 「単に問題が解けたかどうかだけでなく、解くプロセス、説明の明快さ、関連する質問に対する受け答えなどを総合的に評価します」 (not only whether you solved it, but the process, clarity of explanation, and answers to related questions). [V] (S8)
  • The Division page says the Strang 5th edition, Chapters 1 to 8, "is equivalent to Chapter 1 to Chapter 7 if you have the fourth edition." [V] (S5)
  • Official guideline video (S9, from 2020, still linked from S5 and S7). Slide text I read from the video frames:
    • "A device compatible with Webex": a laptop or desktop with camera and mic, or a smartphone ("please prepare a tripod or something that can hold the device").
    • "Sketchbook ... For example, about A4 size". "Prepare thick pen so that it is easy to see the answers from the examiners".
    • "Be prepared in a quiet private room. There should not be any 3rd person. Only the examinee."
    • "In some cases, the examiner can ask to show the room by rotating the camera. Please remove private items in the room."
    • "Please hold the answer so that the examiners can see the solving procedures well." "Please follow the advise and comments from the examiners."
    • "In actual examination, the mathematic problems are shown in English".

3.3 The research part [V]

  • "This examination takes about 12 minutes. We will ask questions related to information science considering your submitted essay and majored field." [V] (S5)
  • "On the day of the examination, we will ask questions about the research proposal and what they learned at the undergraduate institution." [V] (S5)
  • Japanese Q&A: 「提出された小論文を題材として質疑応答が行われます。冒頭口頭でのプレゼンを求めることがあります。」 (Q&A is based on your essay. You may be asked for a short oral presentation at the start.) [V] (S8)
  • 「面接時のプレゼンは口頭で行ってもらいます。機器やプレゼン用の資料、あるいはメモ等の持込みはできません。」 (The presentation is oral only. No devices, slides or notes.) [V] (S8)
  • Dress: 「そのような必要はありません。リラックスできる格好で受験してください。」 (no suit needed; wear something comfortable). [V] (S8) One applicant noticed the examiners wore suits. [A] (A5) [INF] Wear a plain collared shirt.

3.4 Scoring and evaluation [V]

  • Points: 「書類審査50点、英語30点、数学30点、面接90点(小論文を含む)」, total 200. Documents 50, English 30, math 30, interview 90 (the essay is counted inside the interview). 「配点は予告なしに変更されることがあります」 (may change without notice). [V] (S8)
  • Undergraduate grades are inside the 50 document points; 「評価方法については非公開です」 (method not public). [V] (S8)
  • English: the conversion formula is not public. If you submit several tests, each is converted and the highest is used. With no score, English is 0. [V] (S8, S1)
  • In the 2022 exams, the average TOEIC of successful applicants was about 718 (Japanese-language exam) and about 906 (English-language exam). [V] (S8)
  • Acceptance: "an overall evaluation of interview results, the research proposal, school transcripts, TOEIC / TOEFL / IELTS / Duolingo English Test scores, as well as awards and other activities." [V] (S1)
  • Round difficulty: the 1st round has had a lower minimum passing score than rounds 2 and 3, and 「第3回目の入試には社会人などの受験生も多くなり」 (round 3 has many working adults), so it can be harder than the ratio suggests. [V] (S8)
  • Visiting a lab: 「それ自身が直接有利に働くことはありません」 (it is not a direct advantage by itself). [V] (S8)

3.5 Exam day steps [V]

From the Division page (S5, last updated 14 Nov 2022):

  1. "Registration": "the receptionist will confirm the connection and your identity. At the same time, the receptionist will ask you about the status of the examination (exclusive application, concurrent application, first choice, etc.)". So know your answer: is NAIST your only application? Your answer: "NAIST is my only application so far, and Prof. Iida's lab is my first choice." If you apply anywhere else before March, name those universities, then say "NAIST is my first choice." Check first: true only if you have submitted no other application. An email to Nagoya is not an application, but a submitted form is.
  2. "Math examination" (about 12 minutes). Then the math examiners leave.
  3. "Professionalism and research related examination" (about 12 minutes).
  • "II) and III) will take about 30 minutes in total." [V] (S5)

3.9 Math problems past applicants reported [A]

Use these as practice. Rounds and years differ.

  • Integral of (log x)/x from e to e^2. Diagonalize the symmetric matrix [[1,0,2],[0,-1,-2],[2,-2,0]] (find U). (A1, 2023)
  • A positive definite matrix question; the applicant got partial credit for computing the rank. (A2, 2024)
  • A 2x2 cofactor expansion; maximize the volume of a box under a condition. (A4, 2024)
  • A simple projection problem; sketch a hyperbola. (A3, 2025)
  • Convergence of a series; a projection matrix applied to vectors. (A5, 2025)
  • Given det A = -1, find det A^-1, det(-A), det(0.5A), det A^2. For f(x) = sin x / x, find f'(x) and a limit at 0 (L'Hopital). (A6, 2021, 3rd round; full sheet: https://github.com/gabriel6181997/naist_entrance_exam/blob/main/math_exam_questions.pdf)

Tips repeated across reports [A]: say your plan out loud before you start (A4, A5); ask for a hint when stuck, which costs little (A1, A2); know definitions and properties (kernel, orthogonal matrix, eigenvectors) rather than tricks (A1); practice writing with a thick marker (A1).

3.10 Pass marks and ratios [A]

These are self-reported, all 1st round (July), domestic Japanese-language candidates. The official Q&A says rounds 2 and 3 have had higher minimum scores than round 1 (S8), so do not read these as your bar.

  • Minimum passing scores out of 200: 134, 139, 148 (three years before 2023), 144 (2023) (A1); 124 (2024) (A4), while another 2024 report says 120 (A2); 131 (2025) (A5).

4. Your job and the application [V]

The 2027 Guide has a block "Required only for employed applicants *(except for those who will resign before enrollment)". [V] (S1)

5.3 IPGP MEXT (time-critical) [V] (S19)

Exact terms from the "2027 Application Guidelines for MEXT Scholarship for International Priority Graduate Programs (IPGP) - Division of Information Science":

  • "If you wish to apply for this scholarship, please contact the Division of Information Science laboratory you wish to enter."
  • Places: "Master's course 4".
  • "Enrollment date October 1, 2027". "Research students are not accepted under this program."
  • Benefits: "(Master's course) 144,000 yen", "Exemption of tuition and entrance fees", airfare to Japan "upon acceptance" and back "after completing the program". Footnote on the amount: "The amount is subject to change depending on the annual budget of the following year."
  • Term: "From October 2027 to September 2029".
  • Eligibility you meet [INF from brief facts]: nationality with diplomatic relations; "born after April 2, 1992"; 16-year curriculum; English CEFR B2 or higher, with scores "obtained after January 2025" (your IELTS 8.0 of Sep 2026 is C1).
  • Eligibility you must check: "average grade points (*1) of 2.30 or higher". NAIST's table (S20) maps marks to points: 80 to 100 = 3, 70 to 79 = 2, 60 to 69 = 1, 59 and below = 0, weighted by credits, rounded down to two decimals. A CGPA of 8.0/10 does not convert directly; it depends on your marks per course. To do this week: compute it from your transcript marks. Do not state a number before you have computed it.
    • If your university's grades cannot be converted, "please mention in the 'Letter of Recommendation' that the applicant is within the top 30% of the grade ranking." [V] (S19)
  • Not eligible (from the "not eligible" list, items II and III): [V] (S19)
    • II: "Those who have already accepted a scholarship from an organization other than MEXT (including the government of their country)."
    • III: "Those applying for this scholarship through other universities or applying for other support programs for international students from MEXT or Japan Student Services Organization (JASSO)."
    • What this means [INF]: while your IPGP application is open, do not apply for JASSO or any other MEXT program. JASSO is a fallback only if IPGP fails, not a parallel layer. Timing allows this: NAIST's candidate result comes "by December 25, 2026", MEXT's final result "around July" 2027, and the JASSO deadline for October entry is "usually ... in late September" (S23).
  • Documents: application form; field of study and study program; Letter of recommendation ("issued by the Dean, the person corresponding to the Dean, or person of higher authority, and addressed to the President of NAIST"); photo; passport copy; undergraduate transcript; graduation certificate; Evaluation report on applicant ("written by the head or other authorized person of the department", S21); English score; Letter of acceptance from "a prospective supervising professor in NAIST"; abstract of thesis ("If you haven't written graduation thesis yet, summary of research is acceptable").
  • How to submit: "Applicants must send all required documents by e-mail to your prospective supervising professor (Please CC:naist-ipgp[at]is.naist.jp)."
  • Period: "October 14, 2026- November 13, 2026". Results "by December 25, 2026".
  • Screening: "on the basis of the submitted documents. Interviews through internet/e-mail may be conducted if necessary." "Although the formal decision is made by MEXT, applicants' status is essentially guaranteed when they are selected as candidates by NAIST."
  • You still take the same entrance exam: master's "February 8 - 10, 2027" application, "March 10, 2027" exam.

What this means for you [INF]:

  • You need, within about three weeks: a recommendation letter from your college principal or dean (Sri Sairam / VTU), an evaluation report from your department head, your transcript and degree certificate, and Prof. Iida's agreement to write the Letter of Acceptance.
  • To do this week: ask Sri Sairam College of Engineering who can sign the recommendation letter (Dean or Principal) and the evaluation report (department head) before early November.
  • Your decision: October 2027, so IPGP is open to you. "I would prefer October 2027, because it lets me apply for the IPGP scholarship through your lab, but I can start in April 2027 if the lab prefers."
